A comprehensive benchmarking framework for evaluating single-cell foundation models (scFMs) across diverse cancer datasets and downstream tasks, with systematic assessment of biological conservation, batch integration, and classification performance.

Overview
Embedding quality and downstream classification results for single-cell foundation models.

scFM BenchScope is an agentic evaluation framework for single-cell foundation models (scFMs) that systematically assesses both representation quality and performance on clinically relevant tasks. It benchmarks models across biological conservation, batch integration, and predictive performance using diverse cancer datasets and tasks such as cell-type annotation, cancer subtype classification, treatment response prediction, and patient outcome prediction. Beyond benchmarking existing models, BenchScope supports the automated discovery, characterization, and summarization of emerging scFMs, enabling continuous evaluation as new models become available.

Benchmarked models

Models evaluated across biological conservation, batch mixing, and classification:

  • scGPT — Generative pre-trained transformer trained on 33 million human single-cell transcriptomes.
  • Geneformer — BERT-style transformer pre-trained on 30 million human single-cell transcriptomes with rank-value encoding of gene expression.
  • scFoundation — 100-million parameter model pre-trained on 50 million human single cells with an asymmetric transformer architecture.
  • CellPLM — Encodes cell-cell relationships beyond individual cells using a Gaussian mixture latent space.
  • Nicheformer — Joint foundation model for dissociated single-cell and spatial transcriptomics, trained on 110 million cells.
  • Scimilarity — Metric learning foundation model trained on about 22 million single-cell profiles.
  • scConcept — Contrastive pre-training that learns technology-agnostic representations without a reconstruction objective.
  • STATE — Context-aware perturbation model that predicts transcriptional responses to genetic and chemical perturbations.

scFM BenchScope evaluates models on biological conservation, batch mixing, and classification. Together these ask whether embeddings preserve cell-state structure, avoid batch artifacts, and support clinically relevant prediction.

Biological conservation

Whether the embedding preserves biologically meaningful structure, such as cell types, cell states, and neighborhood relationships.

Normalized Mutual Information

Abbreviation: NMI

Agreement between unsupervised clusters and known biological labels, such as annotated cell types. Higher NMI means the embedding organizes cells in a way that better reflects known structure.

Direction: Higher is better.

Adjusted Rand Index

Abbreviation: ARI

Similarity between clustering assignments and known labels, corrected for random agreement. Useful for checking whether cell populations separate into biologically coherent groups.

Direction: Higher is better.

Label Silhouette Score

Abbreviation: ASW Label

How well cells with the same biological label group together compared with cells from different labels. A higher score means stronger separation between populations.

Direction: Higher is better.

Graph Connectivity

Whether cells from the same biological label remain connected in the nearest-neighbor graph. Useful for checking whether embeddings preserve continuous manifolds within each population.

Direction: Higher is better.

Cell-type Local Inverse Simpson’s Index

Abbreviation: cLISI

Local diversity of biological labels in the embedding neighborhood. Low diversity across distinct cell types can mean biologically different populations remain separated.

Direction: Interpretation depends on the task; often lower is better when preserving distinct labels.

kNN Label F1 Score

Uses nearest-neighbor labels to predict each cell’s biological label and reports the F1 score. Higher values mean local neighborhoods are enriched for the same class.

Direction: Higher is better.

kNN Label Accuracy

How accurately a cell’s label can be predicted from its nearest neighbors. Reflects how well the embedding captures local cell-type structure.

Direction: Higher is better.

kNN Label Purity

Fraction of nearest neighbors that share the same biological label as the query cell. High purity means biologically similar cells sit close together.

Direction: Higher is better.

Batch mixing

Whether embeddings reduce technical variation across batches, donors, platforms, or datasets while preserving biological signal.

k-nearest-neighbor Batch Effect Test

Abbreviation: kBET

Tests whether the local batch composition around each cell matches the expected global batch composition. Better kBET performance means batches are well mixed.

Direction: Higher acceptance rate is better.

Integration Local Inverse Simpson’s Index

Abbreviation: iLISI

Local batch diversity in the embedding. A higher iLISI score means cells from different batches mix within local neighborhoods.

Direction: Higher is better.

Batch Silhouette Score

Abbreviation: Batch ASW

Whether cells separate by technical batch. A well-integrated representation should reduce batch-driven separation.

Direction: Lower raw batch separation is better; transformed scores may be reported so higher is better.

Principal Component Regression Batch Score

Abbreviation: PCR Batch

How much variation in the embedding can be explained by batch labels. Lower batch-associated variance suggests better removal of technical effects.

Direction: Lower raw batch effect is better; transformed scores may be reported so higher is better.

kNN Batch Purity

Fraction of nearest neighbors that come from the same batch as the query cell. High batch purity can indicate poor mixing; lower purity suggests better integration.

Direction: Lower is usually better.

Batch Prediction Accuracy

How accurately batch labels can be predicted from the embedding. If batch is easy to predict, the embedding may still contain technical artifacts.

Direction: Lower is usually better.

Classification and clinical-task utility

Whether embeddings support downstream prediction, including patient outcome, treatment response, and other clinical endpoints.

Area Under the Precision–Recall Curve

Abbreviation: AUPRC

Trade-off between precision and recall across thresholds. Especially useful for imbalanced clinical outcomes, where positive cases may be rare.

Direction: Higher is better.

Area Under the Receiver Operating Characteristic Curve

Abbreviation: AUROC / AUC

How well the classifier ranks positive examples above negative examples across thresholds. AUROC summarizes discrimination but may be less informative than AUPRC for highly imbalanced tasks.

Direction: Higher is better.

F1 Score

Harmonic mean of precision and recall. Useful when both false positives and false negatives matter, and when class imbalance makes accuracy less informative.

Direction: Higher is better.

Accuracy

Fraction of correctly classified samples. Intuitive, but can be misleading when outcome classes are imbalanced.

Direction: Higher is better.

Precision

Fraction of predicted positive cases that are truly positive. High precision means fewer false-positive predictions.

Direction: Higher is better.

Recall

Fraction of true positive cases that are correctly identified. High recall means fewer missed positive cases.

Direction: Higher is better.
